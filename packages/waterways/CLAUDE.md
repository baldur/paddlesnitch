# packages/waterways — the river planner

Moved from the root `CLAUDE.md`. The build scripts live in `apps/web/scripts/waterways/` and the `/plan` pages in `apps/web/src/app/plan/`; per-country records are in `docs/features/waterways-*.md`.

## `@paddlesnitch/waterways` (`packages/waterways/src`)

The river planner's domain ([`river-planner.md`](../../docs/features/river-planner.md)). Pure, per-file subpaths:
- `osgb`: OS grid reference and eastings/northings → WGS84 (Helmert, about 5 m).
- `line`: projection onto a line, slice, simplify.
- `network`: snap a click to the water (grid index, `snap(links, p, maxM?)`), shortest route over OSM river/canal links (Dijkstra, costed by `paddleCost`: `boat`/`canoe`=yes at face value, untagged ×2, no ×4, culverts ×50, so a route keeps to the navigation), `reverseRoute` for return trips.
- `along`: features in travel order with chainage and bank (grid-indexed, so a national feature list is cheap).
- `coverage`: which facts each lock has. Unknown stays unknown, never guessed.
- `build`: assemble a region.
- `ingest/{osm,crt,thames}`: parsers for each source.
- `plan`: `routePlan(dataset, { start, finish, shape })` → the route and every stop with chainage and bank, or an error (`start_off_water`, `finish_off_water`, `too_short`, `not_connected`); `timePlan(routed, { paceKmh, portageMinutes, startAt? })` adds arrival times. `computePlan` is both.
- `places`: the places search index (OSM towns and villages within 2 km of the water, plus every named lock and weir) and `searchPlaces`.
- `display`: what the map draws (`overviewLines`, `areaLines` per 0.5° area).
- `checks`: the check routes every national build must pass.
- `portage-detect` (pure): `portagesFromTrack` finds the carry round a lock in a paddler's own track (the slow stretch, < 1.3 m/s, around the closest approach within 80 m; carry 15–500 m, 1–30 min, lock between the landings). tRPC `portage.fromPaddle({ sessionId })` runs it on your own saved paddle, skipping locks you've already described; the paddle page's "Carries on this paddle" (`PortageSuggestions`) draws each (`PortageDrawing`, shared with the print sheet) and publishes only after the CC0 tick. Never published for you.
- `portage` (pure): OpenPortage contributions: two landings per contribution, `portageOnRoute` gives take-out/put-in and banks for the way a route goes, `portagesAt` matches them to a lock or weir (id, or within 80 m). `portage-store` (**server only**): author's copy under `users/{id}/portages/`, public copy under `portages/public/`, flags (3 hide), `erasePortages`. tRPC `portage.add/mine/delete/flag/hide` (hide is platform-admin only, `@paddlesnitch/core/admin`); `plan.route` attaches them to stops; the CC0 dump is `/plan/data/{any}/openportage`.
- `tide` (pure): our own harmonic tide fit (`fitTide`, `tideAt`, `tideExtremes`, `backtest`, `showable`); models in `data/tides.json`, refitted yearly with `scripts/waterways/download-tides.sh` (Thames, EA) + `download-rws-tides.ts` (16 Dutch gauges, `RWS_TIDE_GAUGES`, Rijkswaterstaat measured heights) + `pnpm --filter web waterways:tides`; only gauges passing `showable` are written (the file ships in the planner bundle). `tideWindow` (plan-ui) puts the tide on plans that reach tidal water, from gauges within 25 km of where the route meets it, nearest first; `tideNote` gives the Thames or Dutch (NAP) caveat. Dutch plans prefer Rijkswaterstaat's own predicted high and low waters (`OfficialTides`, `fetch-rws-tide-extremes.ts` in the weekly refresh → published `tides-nl` → `useOfficialTides`, loaded only when `reachesDutchTide`).
- `sun` (pure): sunrise/sunset, `inDark`. `live` (pure) + `live-store` (**server only**): river gauges along a route (EA, SEPA, OPW, Rijkswaterstaat, waterinfo.be for Flanders, PEGELONLINE for Germany with the WSV's own low/normal/high, BAFU for Switzerland (m a.s.l., high past the first flood threshold); matched by river name (ß folds to ss), typical range → low/normal/high; Dutch levels are m NAP with no range, every station's latest fetched in one cached call so no route is sent, archive series dropped by a 6-hour freshness rule) and the Thames stream-warning boards; whole national lists cached per instance, so no route is sent anywhere; tRPC `plan.conditions`; failures listed in `unavailable`, never shown as "no warning".
- `national` (**server only**): `loadNational()` loads the published version named by `data/manifest.json` from storage (`waterways/published/{version}/*.json.gz`) once per instance. With no version published, or locally without it, the committed `data/dw.json` stands in.
- `plan-store` (**server only**, uses core storage): saved plans at `users/{userId}/plans/{id}.json` (with the owner's `notes` and per-stop `stopNotes`, set by `plan.setNotes`; a shared link shows them), so account deletion's `users/` wipe covers them; listed in the account export. `sharePlan`/`unsharePlan`/`getSharedPlan` keep a `plans/shared/{shareId}.json` index; `erasePlanShares` runs on account deletion.

**Coverage: the UK, Ireland, the Netherlands, Belgium, France and Germany**, one dataset (region id `gbie`), with a record per country: [`waterways-uk-ireland.md`](../../docs/features/waterways-uk-ireland.md), [`-netherlands`](../../docs/features/waterways-netherlands.md), [`-belgium`](../../docs/features/waterways-belgium.md), [`-france`](../../docs/features/waterways-france.md), [`-germany`](../../docs/features/waterways-germany.md). Routing runs on the server: tRPC `plan.route({ start, finish, shape })` (public) snaps and routes on the national network and returns stops without times; the browser runs `timePlan`, so pace and start time change instantly. The map loads the network from `GET /plan/data/{version}/overview` (zoomed out) and `…/area-{y}_{x}` (zoomed in), and the GO TO search asks tRPC `plan.places({ q, near })` (server-side `searchPlaces`; the whole index, 15 MB with France, is never sent to the browser). `/plan` keeps an unsaved plan in the address bar (`apps/web/src/lib/plan-ui.ts`); the `plan` router saves plans (region `gbie`, or `dw` for older ones).

National build: download the Geofabrik extracts (`britain-and-ireland`, `netherlands`, `belgium`, `france`, `germany`; CI takes them one at a time for disk), run `apps/web/scripts/waterways/osm_extract.py <pbf> .cache/waterways/osm/{gbie|nl|be|fr|de}` (pyosmium, in a venv), `pnpm --filter web waterways:sources` (every source's features into `.cache/waterways/normalised/{source}.json`: `crt`, `ea-thames`, `ea-aims`, `ea-umap`, `scottish-canals`, `waterways-ireland`, `sepa`, `amber`, `ifi`, `rws-fis`, `nl-waterboards`, `amber-eu` (Belgium and Germany), `roe` (France); fetched through `fetchWithRetry`), then `pnpm --filter web waterways:national` (checks only) or `… --publish` (writes a version to storage and updates `manifest.json`). Never commit a `manifest.json` whose version isn't in the production bucket. Weekly, `.github/workflows/waterways-refresh.yml` does all of this in the `waterways-data` environment with its own role (`att-github-waterways`: only `waterways/*` in the data bucket) then reads the published version back with the server's loader and checks it (`pnpm waterways:verify`: every check route, peak memory ≤ 1700 MB); if that passes it commits `manifest.json` to main and dispatches `deploy.yml` itself, with no PR or human step (since 2026-10-24); any failure opens a "Waterways refresh failed" issue and the site keeps its current version. Roll back by reverting a `data(waterways): publish` commit. It runs `waterways:sources --stage`: each good copy is kept at `waterways/normalised/{source}.json.gz`, and a source that errors, comes back empty or shrinks by over half uses that copy instead (`stale` in `waterways/health.json`, logic in `health.ts`); three runs without a fresh copy opens a "Waterways source X is failing" issue. Its `discover` job (`pnpm waterways:discover`, `discovery.ts`) posts one weekly digest issue of new lock/weir/slipway/canoe datasets on data.gov.ie, OpenDataNI and ArcGIS Online; add used or turned-down sources to `KNOWN` so they don't come back. Coverage: `waterway-coverage.ts` scores every named waterway (locks, portage sides, weirs, access, crew, facilities → good/some/thin) and counts the saved plans that travel it (`plans`, from the route sample each plan's `plans/demand/{planId}.json` keeps — `sampleLine`, ~1 km, ≤400 points; plans first, then length, on `/plan/gaps`); `loadCoverage()` works it out once per server instance; served as `/plan/data/{version}/coverage` and `…/coverage-lines`, shown by the COVERAGE switch on `/plan` and listed on `/plan/gaps`. Parsers: `ingest/{crt,thames,ea-aims,ea-umap,scottish-canals,waterways-ireland,barriers,dbf}`. Points that sit on the watercourse (OSM locks, AMBER, SEPA, EA AIMS, IFI) count on a route only within 60 m when they're in the water (locks, weirs, hazards, portages); bank-side ones (slipways, put-ins, facilities) use the usual 120 m; a weir within 300 m of a lock folds into it (`WEIR_AT_LOCK_M`); weirs from different sources within 40 m merge. OSM lock chambers (`osmLocks`) fill gaps where no authority lists a lock within 120 m; they're published apart as `osm-features.json` (ODbL) and count on a route only within 60 m (`OSM_LOCK_NEAR_M`). OSM portage paths (`portage=*` ways, ends kept by `osm_extract.py`) become landings via `osmPortages` (pieces joined end to end, under 40 m dropped), published as `osm-portages.json` and loaded as `ds.portages`; routing, coverage and "most needed" use them beside contributions, credited "Mapped in OpenStreetMap" (ODbL, never in the CC0 dump; a flight's carry covers every lock within half its length). Public OSM slipways and canoe put-ins (`osmAccess`) fill access gaps the same way: within 60 m of the network, none of ours within 100 m (`GAP_ACCESS_M`), published in `osm-features.json`. Canoe, kayak and paddleboard hire (`amenity=boat_rental` with `canoe_rental`/`kayak_rental`/`standup_paddleboard_rental=yes`, `sport=canoe`, or a canoe/kayak name; pedalos and motorboats not) joins them as access points with `hire: true` ("Canoe and kayak hire."), kept even beside one of ours; so do public OSM toilets and drinking water as facilities (`osmFacilities`, within 100 m of the water; car parks deliberately not used). Where a lock is mapped only by its gates, `osmGateLocks` makes one from two or more `waterway=lock_gate` *nodes* within 60 m with no chamber within 120 m (gate *ways* are skipped: on the tideway they're dock and marina entrances). Share-alike sources (`SOURCES[id].shareAlike`: the Dutch water boards, `nl-waterboards`, CC BY-SA) are published in their own `sa-features.json` and never merged into the core features or the CC0 contributions; water-board points count only within 10 m (`WATERBOARD_NEAR_M`). Waterways Ireland is licensed non-commercial (`SOURCES['waterways-ireland'].nonCommercial`): fine while paddlesnitch is free, never fed into CC0 contributions.

`pnpm --filter web waterways [region]` rebuilds `packages/waterways/data/{region}.json` plus a generated `{region}-coverage.md`, caching raw responses in `apps/web/.cache/waterways/`. The OSM parts (network, points) are ODbL and stay in their own fields, never copied into a `Feature`. Tests: `apps/web/src/lib/waterways.test.ts` (real-response fixtures in `src/tests/fixtures/waterways/`, plus checks on the built DW dataset), `waterways-national.test.ts` (routing by paddle access, grid index, route/time split, places search), `tests/trpc-plan.test.ts` (`plan.route` and the map data route).
