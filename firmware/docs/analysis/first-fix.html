Acquisition

Eighty seconds of nothing, then everything at once

A cold start with no stored almanac has to download orbital data from the satellites themselves before it can compute anything. For 80 seconds the receiver reported zero satellites — indistinguishable, from the outside, from a broken antenna. Then the count climbed almost vertically and the first fix landed within the same second the first satellite was acknowledged.

Satellites trackedFix acquired at t+80 s and held for the remainder of the run

This is why the indoor tests looked broken. Every earlier session ended before the 80-second mark or ran without sky view, so the log showed nothing but zeros — the same signature a dead GNSS module produces.

Fix quality

Horizontal precision was excellent; vertical was not

HDOP measures how favourably the satellites are arranged for a horizontal fix — below 1.0 is considered excellent. It settled at 0.8 and stayed there. Altitude tells a different story: it wandered across a 54-metre range while the receiver was physically motionless, which is normal and expected. GNSS vertical error is routinely two to three times the horizontal error, because every satellite in view is above the receiver and none below it.

Position

A 115-metre walk that never left a tennis court

Summing the distance between consecutive fixes gives 115 metres. The actual bounding box of every fix is roughly 33 × 10 metres. Both numbers are correct: the receiver was near-stationary, and what accumulated was per-sample scatter, not travel. It is a useful reminder that naive point-to-point summing inflates distance badly at low speed — a real tracker needs a movement threshold before it counts a step.

The speed trace confirms it: 374 of 421 fixed samples read under 1 km/h. The isolated spikes above 6 km/h line up with rotation peaks on the gyroscope — the device being turned over in a hand, not carried anywhere.

Motion

The accelerometer saw the walk the GPS almost missed

Sampled at 50 Hz and reduced to a per-second peak, the accelerometer registers handling and footfall that the 1 Hz position trace flattens entirely. Peak magnitude reaches 2.83 g against a 1.0 g resting baseline. This is the channel that would let a real tracker decide when it is worth spending a LoRa transmission — and, just as usefully, when to stay quiet.
