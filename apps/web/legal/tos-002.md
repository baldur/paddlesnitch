## 2. What the Service does

The Service does three things:

- **Time trials.** Organisers set up river courses; paddlers and rowers upload GPS files; the Service works out times, splits and leaderboards.
- **Paddles.** You can add your own paddles (from a file, Strava, a time trial or a tracker) and see an analysis of each one: speed, stroke rate, rests, efforts, and the wind and river flow that day.
- **Trackers.** You can link a paddlesnitch tracker, which uploads its recordings on its own.

Paddles get a short summary written automatically by an AI model from your paddle's numbers, your earlier paddles and your diary notes. **The summary can be wrong.** Treat it as a suggestion, not coaching advice.

- Timing is **amateur**. The Service is not a regulated race-timing system. Do not use it for official competition results without a separate human verification step.
- GPS data quality varies between devices and conditions. Times can be inaccurate.
- Leaderboards reflect what users submitted; we do not independently verify identities or boat configurations.

**Important.** When you submit to a time trial, the trial's organiser may at any future point choose to make that trial **public**. If they do, your name + computed time + per-split breakdown becomes visible to anyone on the internet, including search engines.

- Submitting to a **public** trial = your performance is public immediately.
- Submitting to a **private** or **group-scoped** trial = your performance is visible only within that scope **at the time of submission**, but the organiser can later make the trial public. By submitting, you accept that this can happen.

You can request removal of your entries from any trial by emailing privacy@paddlesnitch.com. We will action removal requests within a reasonable timeframe, normally one month under UK GDPR Art. 17.
